## 1. WHAT IS THE SOVEREIGN FRAMEWORK?

The Sovereign Framework is a system of 18 interconnected skills (tools) that solve one problem at every scale: **turning what is idle, wasted, or untapped into profitable, sustainable economic activity that creates jobs, builds wealth, expands the tax base, and makes communities and governments self-sustaining.**

The formula is simple:

```
IDLE ASSETS + ORGANISED PEOPLE + MARKET CONNECTION = WEALTH + JOBS + TAXPAYERS
```

Every skill starts from the same principle: **audit what already exists before asking for anything new.** A community has land, skills, savings groups, natural resources, and social structures. A government ministry has buildings, staff, budgets, data, and legal mandates. A struggling business has equipment, customers, relationships, and a brand. The Framework makes what is invisible, visible — and then activates it.

## 2. THE FIVE SOVEREIGN DOCTRINES

These are the non-negotiable principles that govern every skill in the Framework:

| # | Doctrine | What It Means In Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sovereign Assets First** | Before designing anything, audit what already exists. The enterprise/programme is built on what the community, government, or business already has — not on what it needs to be given. |
| 2 | **Market Revenue Mandate** | Every solution must generate private market income. No single revenue source (including government) should exceed 60% of total revenue by Year 3. This is how you survive budget cuts. |
| 3 | **People as Delivery** | The unemployed are not the problem — they are the workforce. Three populations are always available: unemployed graduates, long-term unemployed adults, and retired professionals. |
| 4 | **Profitable & Provable** | Every programme must be self-sustaining within 24 months, or its social value must be proven with financial rigour. No vague "impact" claims — numbers or nothing. |
| 5 | **Evidence-Based Everything** | Every figure in every document must be verified against official sources. Exchange rates checked same-day. Government budget from current year. No stale data. |

## 3. HOW TO USE THE SKILLS

### With Claude Code (Anthropic's AI CLI)
Place the skill files from the `skills/` folder into `~/.claude/commands/` and invoke them with slash commands:
```
/solve [describe your problem]
/opportunity-scan [geography and sector]
/community-enterprise [community description and enterprise idea]
/financial-model [programme or business name]
```

### With Other AI Platforms (ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.)
1. Open the relevant `.md` file from `skills/`
2. Copy the full contents
3. Paste it as the first message or system prompt
4. Replace `$ARGUMENTS` with your specific input
5. The AI will follow the skill's structure and produce the output

### Important Usage Notes
- **You provide the input.** Each skill has a `$ARGUMENTS` section where you describe your specific situation — the community, the business, the problem, the geography.
- **If your input is incomplete, the skill will ask questions.** Every skill has a questioning protocol that asks for the missing pieces before proceeding.
- **Skills can be chained.** The output of one skill feeds into the next. For example: `/asset-audit` identifies what exists → `/gov-proposal` uses those assets in a government proposal → `/financial-model` builds the full economics.
- **Always run `/data-verify` first** when producing any document with financial figures for government or investors.

### 5.1 `/solve` — THE MASTER CONSULTANT ORCHESTRATOR
**File:** `skills/solve.md`

**What it does:** This is the brain of the Framework. You give it any complex, messy, multi-layered problem — and it diagnoses the problem, selects the right combination of other skills, puts them in the correct sequence, and produces an integrated solution.

**Who it's for:** Anyone facing a problem that spans multiple dimensions — policy, finance, people, infrastructure, community. Government ministries, programme directors, community leaders, struggling businesses.

**What you give it:** A problem description. Can be vague ("youth unemployment in the Northern Region") or specific ("Ministry of Employment has 47 dormant TVET centres, 12,000 registered unemployed graduates, and a GHS 50 million budget they can't spend").

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Problem Diagnosis** — What is actually happening, across 5 dimensions (nature, geography, urgency, complexity, audience)
2. **Three Critical Questions** answered — What assets exist? What is the path to self-sufficiency? Where does the workforce come from?
3. **Skill Selection** — Which skills to use and in what order
4. **Full Integrated Solution** — Using a 3-layer architecture:
   - Foundation Layer (assets and infrastructure)
   - Engine Layer (people and delivery)
   - Sustainability Layer (revenue and growth)
5. **Critical Path** — Numbered steps with owners and timelines
6. **Risk Register** — What could go wrong and how to prevent it
7. **One-Page Master Brief** — Visual summary

**Key rules:**
- Never recommends external funding as a starting point
- Always starts with what already exists (sovereign asset audit)
- Market revenue is embedded from Day 1
- Every solution must have a self-sustaining financial model

**When to use:** When the problem is too big or complex for a single skill. When you don't know which skill to start with. When the solution needs to combine government, community, workforce, and financial elements.

### 5.2 `/asset-audit` — SOVEREIGN ASSET STRATEGIST
**File:** `skills/asset-audit.md`

**What it does:** Audits EVERYTHING a government, organisation, or community already owns — and calculates how much idle value is sitting there unused. This is always the first step before designing any programme or enterprise.

**Who it's for:** Government ministries preparing new programmes. Local authorities. Communities. Any organisation that says "we don't have the resources" — this skill proves they do.

**What you give it:** Country, ministry or organisation, geographic region, and what you're trying to do.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Physical Asset Inventory** — Land, buildings, equipment, vehicles (with location, current use, % utilisation, and repurposing options)
2. **Human Capital Asset Inventory** — Existing staff who could be redeployed, retired professionals available, community structures (SUSU groups, women's associations, youth groups)
3. **Programmatic Asset Inventory** — Active and dormant government programmes, legal mandates, existing data and research
4. **Relational Asset Inventory** — Government contacts, NGO relationships, community trust networks
5. **Financial Asset Inventory** — Unspent budgets, revenue-generating assets not being monetised
6. **Asset Utilisation Matrix** — Every asset rated 0–100% used, with repurposing priority
7. **Repurposing Blueprint** — Specific plan for each high-priority underutilised asset
8. **Total Value Unlocked** — Combined value in local currency of what can be activated immediately
9. **90-Day Quick Wins** — Actions with zero or near-zero cost that prove value immediately
10. **One-Page Brief** — Visual summary

**The key insight:** The phrase "we don't have the budget" almost always means "we haven't audited what we already have." This skill makes the invisible visible.

**When to use:** ALWAYS first. Before any programme design, business plan, government proposal, or investment case. The asset audit reduces capital requirements by showing what already exists.

### 5.3 `/opportunity-scan` — ECONOMIC INTELLIGENCE ANALYST
**File:** `skills/opportunity-scan.md`

**What it does:** Scans any geography — a district, a region, a country — and identifies every viable business opportunity that can be started using what already exists in that place.

**Who it's for:** Government economic planners. Private entrepreneurs looking for opportunities. Community groups wondering what enterprise to start. Diaspora investors looking for where to invest back home.

**What you give it:** A geography (country, region, district), optionally a sector focus, who will act on the opportunities, and what assets are already known.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Economic Baseline** — Current income sources, what the area imports and exports, unemployment, business density
2. **Eight Categories of Opportunities:**
   - **Import Substitution** — What does this area buy from outside that could be made locally?
   - **Value Addition** — What raw products leave unprocessed that could be processed here?
   - **Underserved Markets** — Who has money but can't find a local supplier?
   - **Government Spending** — What does government spend money on that local businesses could supply?
   - **Natural Resources** — Forests, rivers, clay, medicinal plants — how to commercialise?
   - **Skills-Based Services** — What can local people do that others will pay for?
   - **Digital/Platform Economy** — Phone-based work with minimal startup
   - **Circular Economy** — Waste into resource
3. **Opportunity Ranking** — Top 5 opportunities scored by revenue potential, capital required, jobs created, tax revenue, and barrier to entry
4. **Implementation Gateway** — 90-day action plan for the top-ranked opportunities
5. **One-Page Brief** — Visual summary for government and investors

**When to use:** Before choosing an enterprise for a community. Before designing a government economic programme. When an entrepreneur asks "what business should I start in this area?" When a district assembly wants to know what economic potential exists.

### 5.5 `/workforce-engine` — HUMAN CAPITAL TRANSFORMATION STRATEGIST
**File:** `skills/workforce-engine.md`

**What it does:** Designs the complete delivery workforce for any programme — matching every role to one of 3 underutilised populations, showing training pathways, income models, and the trajectory from trainee to enterprise owner.

**Who it's for:** Programme directors who need to staff a programme. Government HR planners. NGO programme managers.

**The 3 Populations (this is central to the Framework):**

| Population | Who They Are | What They Bring | Pathway |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unemployed Graduates** | Degree/diploma holders, 18–35, digitally literate, no work experience | Education, energy, digital skills, ambition | 8–10 week fast-track → programme roles → enterprise owner |
| **Long-Term Unemployed** | Adults 25–55, limited formal education, community-embedded, practical skills | Community trust, local knowledge, practical skills, resilience | 8-week re-entry → community roles → cooperative leader |
| **Retired Professionals** | Former civil servants, teachers, nurses, engineers, 55+ | Decades of expertise, government networks, institutional memory, credibility | 2-week onboarding → advisory/mentorship roles → consulting practice |

**What you give it:** Programme name, country, geographic scale, existing staff, private sector present, budget context.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Programme Mirror** — Every function the programme needs, with current cost vs. local alternative cost
2. **Workforce Strategy** for each of the 3 populations — roles, training, income progression, enterprise pathway
3. **Integrated Org Structure** — How all three populations work together
4. **Market Revenue from Workforce** — How programme staff generate private income (NGO contracts, agribusiness, telecom agent work, private training clients)
5. **Geographic Scaling Model** — Sub-district → district → regional → national, funded by profit at each level
6. **Enterprise Ecosystem** — 7 types of enterprises that emerge from the workforce (data firms, cooperatives, training academies, etc.)
7. **Human Impact Stories** — 3 named narratives showing the journey from unemployed to enterprise owner
8. **One-Page Brief** — Visual summary

**Key insight:** Government saves 40–60% vs. foreign contractors, and the workforce becomes self-sustaining (they don't need permanent government salary — they build their own enterprises).

**When to use:** When any programme needs staff. When government wants to create jobs through programme delivery. When designing how a community enterprise will be staffed and managed.

### 5.6 `/grad-bridge` — EDUCATION-TO-ECONOMY INTEGRATION SPECIALIST
**File:** `skills/grad-bridge.md`

**What it does:** Bridges the gap between the final year of education and economic life. The goal is zero unemployment on graduation day — every student leaves with either a job offer, a government programme placement, or a registered business.

**Who it's for:** Universities, polytechnics, TVET centres, secondary schools with vocational tracks, education ministries.

**What you give it:** Institution type and location, student cohort size, fields of study, graduation timeline, government programmes in the area, private sector present.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Current State Diagnosis** — The education-to-unemployment gap in numbers
2. **Opportunity Matching by Field of Study** — For each field (agriculture, nursing, engineering, ICT, education, business, fashion, hospitality): which government programme, which employer, which business opportunity, and what Day 1 income looks like
3. **Final-Year Bridge Curriculum** — 12-month programme running alongside normal studies, month by month
4. **Institution Partnership Model** — MoU structure and fee options
5. **Student Journey Narrative** — One named student's full pathway from Month 12 of school to Day 1 of work
6. **Pre-Graduation Pipeline Summary** — Target: 80%+ of students have confirmed placements before graduation

**Key insight:** Students register businesses, connect with government programmes, and secure placements BEFORE graduation — not after. The bridge runs during the final year, not after it.

**When to use:** When working with educational institutions. When a community wants its young people employed. When a government programme needs graduate-level staff.

**What you give it:** Community location, population, enterprise idea (or request for help choosing), available assets (land, water, skills, SUSU groups, buildings, market relationships), current income sources, governance structures (chief, women's group, youth association), market access, community champion identified, starting capital.

3. **Enterprise Structure and Governance** — Legal structure recommendation (cooperative vs. CBO vs. LLC), governance framework (who manages, who oversees, how profits are distributed, how disputes are resolved), SUSU integration
4. **Financial Model** — Startup costs (asset-first, showing what's free), revenue model (specific buyers named), cost structure, break-even, member income projections Month 1 through Year 2+
5. **Market Connections** — Local and regional buyers identified, export pathway, government programme supply opportunities
6. **Training and Launch Plan** — 8-week pre-launch training, 90-day launch milestones (Day 1: community meeting → Day 60: first revenue)
7. **Wealth Building Model** — Year-by-year member income growth, cooperative reserve accumulation, community investments (Year 3: equipment → Year 5: community hall/water system/school support)
8. **One-Page Enterprise Brief** — Visual summary

**Key features:**
- No external funding required — built on community assets and SUSU savings
- SUSU group becomes the enterprise governance and dividend mechanism
- Government programme links (School Feeding, Planting for Food and Jobs) as anchor contracts
- Member income projections tied to verified commodity prices
- Wealth accumulates in the community, not extracted by outsiders

**When to use:** When a community wants to start a collective enterprise. When a district assembly wants community economic development. When an NGO wants to support community self-sufficiency.

### 5.10 `/business-rescue` — BUSINESS TURNAROUND SPECIALIST
**File:** `skills/business-rescue.md`

**What it does:** Diagnoses why a struggling business is failing and prescribes a structured 90-day rescue plan. The core insight: most "failures" are actually asset misallocation problems — the business has assets, customers, and products, but they're arranged wrong.

**Who it's for:** Struggling businesses, family enterprises, cooperatives losing money, lenders considering foreclosure, anyone asking "should we close this or save it?"

**What you give it:** Business description (type, location, what's gone wrong, how long, how bad), owner commitment level, existing assets, debts, and any government programme connection.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Health Diagnostic** — 10 dimensions rated 1–10 with overall health score and rescue verdict (rescue viable / rescue with conditions / wind down with dignity)
2. **Root Cause Analysis** — Which of 7 failure modes is this? (Revenue concentration? Pricing too low? Working capital trap? Market obsolescence? Founder dependency? Cost mismatch? Unstructured growth?)
3. **Asset Audit of the Business** — What the business owns that's being underutilised
4. **Revenue Redesign** — Restructured revenue streams (anchor revenue, government contract, new market, value-added products, idle asset monetisation)
5. **Cost Restructure** — What to cut without killing revenue-generation capacity
6. **People Redesign** — Current team assessment; 3-population injection if needed
7. **Government Programme Connection** — Government contract as fastest revenue stabiliser
8. **Debt Restructure** — Creditor proposals using the rescue plan as evidence of viability
9. **90-Day Rescue Plan** — Week-by-week actions
10. **One-Page Rescue Brief** — Visual summary

**When to use:** When a business is losing money. When considering whether to close or save. When a lender wants to see if rescue is viable before foreclosure.

### 5.11 `/financial-model` — DEVELOPMENT FINANCE SPECIALIST
**File:** `skills/financial-model.md`

**What it does:** Builds the complete financial architecture for any programme or enterprise — revenue streams, cost structure, P&L, cash flow, break-even, sensitivity analysis, and geographic scaling economics.

**Who it's for:** Programme directors needing budget approval. Enterprise founders needing investor-grade financials. Government officials assessing programme viability. Loan officers assessing creditworthiness.

**What you give it:** Programme or business name, country, geographic scale, government contract value (if any), staff numbers, known private buyers, community payment capacity.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Revenue Architecture** — Three streams (government + market + community) with monthly projections Year 1 and annual projections Years 2–5
2. **Cost Structure** — Personnel, infrastructure (offset by sovereign assets), training, operations, overhead
3. **Profit and Loss** — Monthly P&L Year 1; annual P&L Years 2–5
4. **Cash Flow Projection** — Critical for Africa: profit does not equal cash (government pays 60–120 days late)
5. **Break-Even Analysis** — When the programme covers its own costs (target: Month 12–18)
6. **Return on Investment** — Financial ROI for government; SROI for social outcomes
7. **Sensitivity Analysis** — What if government cuts budget? What if market revenue grows slower? Can it survive 6-month payment delays?
8. **Geographic Scaling Model** — How the economics work from sub-district to national
9. **Operational Documents** — Monthly management report template, KPI dashboard, decision rules (when to freeze spend, when to chase payment, when to activate emergency pipeline)
10. **One-Page Financial Summary** — Visual for government presentation

**Key rules:**
- Government revenue capped at 60% by Year 3
- Market revenue must reach 35%+ by Year 2
- 90-day cash reserve required (government payment delays are standard)
- Exchange rate checked same-day; inflation buffer built into multi-year projections

**When to use:** After any enterprise design, programme proposal, or business plan is drafted. The financial model validates whether the idea actually works in numbers.

### 5.12 `/social-value` — SOCIAL VALUE ECONOMIST
**File:** `skills/social-value.md`

**What it does:** Quantifies the full social value of a programme that cannot fully pay for itself through market revenue. Uses rigorous Social Return on Investment (SROI) methodology to prove that the programme's social benefits are worth more than the subsidy it requires.

**Who it's for:** Government officials under pressure to cut programme funding. NGOs defending social programmes. Impact investors measuring returns. Policy makers choosing between competing budget priorities.

**What you give it:** Programme name, what it does, why it can't be fully commercial, who benefits, the cost of the programme not existing, and whether government currently supports or opposes it.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Social Purpose Statement** — Why this programme exists + cost of non-existence table
2. **Intangible Benefit Identification** — 11 categories: health, education, social cohesion, mental health, gender equality, crime reduction, migration reduction, environment, cultural preservation, institutional trust, diaspora engagement
3. **Benefit Monetisation** — 7 methods to put a currency value on each benefit
4. **SROI Calculation** — "For every GHS 1 invested, GHS X of social value is returned" (with attribution and deadweight adjustments)
5. **Policy Justification** — 5 arguments for government: false economy of cutting, replacement cost, irreversibility, intergenerational impact, political risk
6. **Limited Revenue Pathways** — Even social programmes can generate some revenue (micro-fees, data products, training certification)
7. **10-Year Projection** — Cumulative SROI showing long-term compounding value
8. **One-Page Brief** — Visual summary

**Key insight:** This skill does not apologise for programmes that can't fully pay for themselves. It QUANTIFIES their value in the language finance committees speak: numbers. "Cutting this programme doesn't save money — it transfers costs to other budget lines at higher cost."

**When to use:** When a valuable programme faces budget cuts. When justifying government subsidy. When an impact investor needs measurable social returns. When policy makers need evidence to choose.

### 5.13 `/human-capital-profile` — HUMAN CAPITAL DEVELOPMENT SPECIALIST
**File:** `skills/human-capital-profile.md`

**What it does:** Takes a specific population group and profiles them completely — who they are, what hidden assets they carry, how to activate them, and the full economic trajectory from training to enterprise ownership.

**Who it's for:** Government workforce planners. Programme directors recruiting from a specific population. Educators designing curricula. Anyone asking "what can these people actually do?"

**What you give it:** Country, region, population description (e.g., "350 TVET graduates in Brong-Ahafo with electrical training but no jobs"), programme context, and private sector presence.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Asset-First Population Audit** — What hidden assets this group already carries (before any training)
2. **Population Portrait** — Demographics, current survival strategies, digital access, financial situation, psychological state
3. **Role Architecture** — Full spectrum of roles from entry to enterprise, matched to this specific population
4. **Training Pathway** — Weekly curriculum, delivery model, African context accommodations (childcare, transport, literacy, seasonal calendar, gender, religious observance)
5. **Income and Enterprise Model** — Income by stage (stipend → salary → business → independent enterprise), 6–10 specific micro-business ideas this population can start, cooperative formation plan
6. **Community and Family Impact** — Ripple effects: dependents fed, school attendance, healthcare access, community leadership, gender dynamics
7. **Retention System** — Why people leave programmes and how to prevent it; recognition system; alumni network
8. **Geographic Deployment** — Sub-district through national, funded by profit at each level
9. **Government Pitch** — The argument, the specific ask, the recommended pilot
10. **One-Page Brief** — Visual summary

**Key insight:** Training is ADDITIVE — it only fills gaps. The profile identifies what skills, knowledge, and networks the population already has, so training doesn't waste time teaching what people already know.

**When to use:** Before designing any training programme. When deploying a specific population into a programme. When you need deep understanding of a community's human resources.

### 5.17 `/data-verify` — DATA INTEGRITY GUARDIAN
**File:** `skills/data-verify.md`

**What it does:** The quality gate. Ensures no deliverable with economic, financial, or policy claims leaves the Framework without data verification against current official sources. This is the skill that protects the consultancy's credibility and prevents budget errors.

**Who it's for:** Quality control for ALL other skills. Run this BEFORE producing any government proposal, financial model, or investor document.

**What you give it:** Country, sector, and type of deliverable being produced.

**What it gives you back:**
1. **Data Confidence Report** — Every data point rated:
   - 🟢 HIGH CONFIDENCE (2025–2026 verified) — use freely
   - 🟡 MEDIUM CONFIDENCE (2023–2024) — use with caveat and 15% variance buffer
   - 🔴 LOW CONFIDENCE (2021–2022) — flag or replace
   - ❌ UNACCEPTABLE (pre-2021) — never use
2. **Stale Data Flags** — Each outdated figure with replacement source
3. **Budget and Liability Risks** — Figures that could cause contract losses if wrong
4. **Data Gaps** — What couldn't be verified online and needs field verification
5. **Overall Assessment** — Data confidence score (X/10), liability risk level, recommendation (proceed / proceed with disclaimers / do not proceed)

**Absolute Red Lines (work STOPS until resolved):**
- Exchange rate older than 7 days
- Government budget from prior fiscal year
- Import/export data older than 3 years
- Unemployment rate without year stated
- Commodity prices without date

**When to use:** ALWAYS before producing any document with financial figures. Before any government submission. Before any investor document.

## 6. HOW THE SKILLS WORK TOGETHER

The 18 skills form an integrated system. Here are the common workflows:

### The Master Sequence (used by `/solve`)
```
/data-verify → /asset-audit → /gov-proposal or /workforce-engine → /financial-model → /social-value (if needed)
```

### By Use Case

**A community wants to start an enterprise:**
```
/opportunity-scan [geography]
    ↓ identifies best enterprise options
/community-enterprise [community + chosen enterprise]
    ↓ designs the enterprise, governance, financial model
/financial-model [enterprise name]
    ↓ detailed P&L, cash flow, break-even
/social-value [if programme has non-commercial benefits]
    ↓ quantifies social return
/crowd-fund [if external capital needed to scale]
```

**A graduate has a business idea:**
```
/idea-engine [the idea]
    ↓ validates and develops the business plan
/financial-model [business name]
    ↓ detailed financials
/pitch [to investors or first clients]
    ↓ sales and engagement strategy
/crowd-fund [if capital needed]
```

**A government ministry wants a new programme:**
```
/data-verify [country, sector]
    ↓ ensures all data is current
/asset-audit [ministry assets]
    ↓ maps what already exists
/workforce-engine [programme staffing]
    ↓ designs the delivery workforce
/gov-proposal [the full proposal]
    ↓ ready-to-submit document
/financial-model [programme economics]
    ↓ validates financial viability
/pitch [to minister or decision-maker]
```

**A business is struggling:**
```
/business-rescue [business description]
    ↓ diagnosis and 90-day rescue plan
/financial-model [restructured business]
    ↓ validates new revenue model
/opportunity-scan [local area]
    ↓ identifies new revenue opportunities
```

**An educational institution wants to reduce graduate unemployment:**
```
/grad-bridge [institution + student cohort]
    ↓ designs the final-year bridge programme
/opportunity-scan [local area]
    ↓ identifies opportunities for graduates
/human-capital-profile [graduate population]
    ↓ deep profile of what graduates can do
/workforce-engine [deploying graduates into programmes]
```

**A consultancy needs to pitch to a client:**
```
/pitch [client type + problem]
    ↓ complete engagement package
/asset-audit [Discovery phase]
    ↓ first deliverable that proves value
/gov-proposal [formal submission]
    ↓ the full proposal
```
